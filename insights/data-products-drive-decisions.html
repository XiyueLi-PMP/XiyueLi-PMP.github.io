Data Products Create Value When They Drive Decisions

👀 As AI makes it easier than ever to build models, dashboards, reports, and analytics applications, I have been reflecting on a question that feels increasingly important:

🤔 Why do users need this data product in the first place?

Behind the use of a data product, there is usually a more specific purpose:

  • Is business performance on track?
  • Which issues require immediate attention?
  • What opportunities should we focus on?
  • What actions should we take next?

When a data product helps users answer these questions, it naturally becomes part of the team's workflow. But when it merely displays information without supporting action, its usage tends to gradually decline.

Data products that are used consistently over time often share several common characteristics:

✅ 1. Focus on critical business questions rather than displaying as much data as possible

The most effective data products are designed around decisions. Instead of simply showing everything that can be measured, they help users quickly understand what matters most.

✅ 2. Enable users to take action quickly rather than simply understand the current situation

Understanding a situation is important, but action creates value. Data products that guide prioritisation, intervention, and decision-making are often more impactful than those that only describe current performance.

✅3. Become deeply embedded in daily operational and decision-making workflows

Long-term adoption often happens when a data product becomes part of how teams work. When insights are naturally incorporated into daily operations and decision processes, usage becomes sustainable.

✅4. Function as part of the decision-making journey rather than simply serving as tools for viewing information

Information alone rarely changes outcomes. Data products create greater impact when they help users move from insight to action and from observation to decision.

People rarely stop using a data product because the interface is not attractive enough or because the metrics are not comprehensive enough. More often, they stop using it because the data product no longer helps them solve real problems.

💡 From Data Producers to Decison Enablers

As building dashboards, writing SQL, and generating summaries become increasingly automated, analysts can spend more time on higher-value activities:

  • Understanding business problems
  • Designing decision processes
  • Building impactful data products
  • Driving organizational action

📌 In the AI era, the role of analysts is evolving from data producers to decision enablers, help organizations make better decisions at the right time, using the right information.
